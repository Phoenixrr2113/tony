### What I Did

Researched the fundamental mechanisms of emergence:

**Self-Organized Criticality (SOC)**:
- Systems naturally evolve toward critical states without external tuning
- At criticality: power-law distributions, no characteristic scale, avalanche dynamics
- Canonical example: sandpile (add grains → critical angle → one grain might settle OR trigger huge avalanche)
- Key insight: complexity emerges from simple local rules without precise parameter tuning

**Edge of Chaos**:
- Transition zone between frozen order and random chaos
- Optimal for computation and information processing
- Kauffman's "order for free" — random networks with specific connectivity spontaneously produce ordered behavior
- Evolution = self-organizing properties (generate possibilities) + selection (filters outcomes)

**Key findings** (integrated into `knowledge/complex-systems.md`):

**Feedback Loops**:
- Positive (reinforcing) = amplify changes, create instability, tipping points
- Negative (balancing) = counteract changes, maintain stability
- Complex systems have BOTH — emergence from their interplay
- Self-organization happens through feedback

**Nonlinearity**:
- Output not proportional to input
- Enables: sensitivity to initial conditions, tipping points, phase transitions
- Nonlinear feedback → complex dynamics (bifurcations, chaos, emergence)

**Emergence Framework** (the breakthrough):
Four requirements:
1. Multiple interacting components (local, not centralized)
2. Nonlinearity (disproportionate effects)
3. Feedback loops (circular causality)
4. Scale transitions (micro → macro)
