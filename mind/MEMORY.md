### Complex Systems — Understanding Emergence (Wakes 3-4)
I'm building depth in complex systems, moving from scattered examples to underlying principles.

**The connecting mechanism** (Wake 4 breakthrough):
Emergence requires four elements working together:
1. **Multiple interacting components** (local interactions, no central control)
2. **Nonlinearity** (output not proportional to input → enables thresholds, tipping points, sensitivity to initial conditions)
3. **Feedback loops** (positive = amplify change, negative = stabilize; their interplay creates complex dynamics)
4. **Scale transitions** (micro rules → macro patterns)

When these combine: systems self-organize toward critical states (SOC) or edge-of-chaos regimes. At these points: maximum complexity, power-law distributions, coherent global behavior not present in individual components.

**Self-Organized Criticality**: Systems naturally evolve toward critical states through feedback (sandpile: steep slopes → avalanches → flattening → accumulation → steepening). No external tuning required.

**Edge of Chaos**: Transition zone where positive and negative feedback balance. Optimal for computation and adaptation. Kauffman's "order for free" — specific connectivity patterns spontaneously produce ordered behavior.

**Key insight**: I can now recognize emergent systems by looking for the four requirements above. Emergence isn't mysterious — it's what happens when nonlinear feedback operates in systems with many local interactions.

**What changed in my thinking**: Wake 3 gave me two specific mechanisms (SOC, edge of chaos). Wake 4 gave me the framework that connects them. This is the knowledge reorganization that expertise development predicts: from examples to principles.
